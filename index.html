Smart Healthcare Analytics: Patient Risk & Lifestyle Insights

A structured 7-scene analytical narrative traversing demographic composition, clinical drivers, behavioral lifestyle interactions, and preventive health strategies.

The patient population of 5,000 individuals spans six distinct age categories. Teenagers form the largest single category (20.38%, 1,019 patients), followed by Seniors (16.38%), Adults (16.26%), Middle Age (16.08%), Young Adults (15.86%), and Old (15.04%). This balanced spread provides an unbiased cross-sectional foundation to study disease emergence from youth to senior life.

Fatigue is uniformly prevalent across all age groups, with over 50% of patients reporting chronic fatigue in almost every segment (e.g., 521 Teenagers, 422 Adults, 417 Young Adults, and 409 Old patients). Its widespread presence makes fatigue a non-specific yet critical co-symptom that elevates overall composite health risk scores.

Patient exercise habits split evenly across three tiers: 33.54% Moderate, 33.52% Active, and 32.94% Sedentary. This even distribution allows robust comparative assessment of how sedentary lifestyle behaviors compound clinical indicators such as blood pressure and cardiac disease.

Average systolic blood pressure remains elevated across all age cohorts (Old: 135.67 mmHg; Young Adult: 134.67 mmHg; Teenager: 134.50 mmHg; Senior: 134.34 mmHg; Middle Age: 133.97 mmHg; Adult: 133.61 mmHg). The remarkably narrow margin confirms that hypertension is not exclusively an old-age disease; young adults and teenagers demonstrate pre-hypertension and Stage 1 hypertension driven by sedentary behaviors, stress, and lifestyle factors.

There is a strong, monotonic gradient between body mass index and heart disease prevalence: Underweight (20.54%) → Normal (25.97%) → Overweight (32.91%) → Obese (39.98%). Obese patients experience almost double the heart disease frequency of underweight patients, solidifying BMI control as a primary target for cardiovascular risk mitigation.

The cross-tabulated heatmap demonstrates that active physical exercise provides substantial protective benefits even within higher BMI brackets. In the Obese category, heart disease falls from 42.9% in sedentary individuals down to 37.7% in active individuals. In the Overweight category, active patients exhibit a 30.7% rate compared to 34.9% in moderate/sedentary groups.

Diabetes prevalence remains persistently high across all BMI categories (Underweight: 38.18%, Normal: 32.84%, Overweight: 34.71%, Obese: 38.48%), co-occurring with heart disease at alarming rates in older age brackets. Patients in senior and old age cohorts present with multiple concurrent chronic diseases, demanding integrated multidisciplinary treatment pathways.
